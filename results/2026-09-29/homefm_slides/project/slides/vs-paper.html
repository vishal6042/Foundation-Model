DomusFM, the baseline

How our DomusFM differs from the paper, in plain words

From the paper (added in run 3)

Clean the data: drop repeated ON/ON or OFF/OFF events (10 % of events)

Test on the paper's own datasets: Kasteren A, Kasteren C, MuRAL

Leave each test dataset out of its own pretraining (5 models)

Our own changes

The six game fixes (slide 12)

Far more pretraining data: 77 CASAS homes (the paper used 2)

6 extra test homes from CASAS

Guessed settings the paper does not give (28.6M vs 36.1M parameters)

Stricter testing

Test on a later time than training. The paper probably splits at random, which lets near-copies of test windows into training

The 6 CASAS test homes are never seen in any pretraining

Random split only as a check, to compare with the paper

What it means: our absolute scores are lower than the paper's because our test is harder, not because the model is weaker. The gain from pretraining for activity recognition, which the stricter test measures fairly, is as large as the paper's or larger on 3 of the 4 shared datasets.

Smaller, to save time: 3 test splits (paper 5), 5 % and 30 % labels (paper 5–30 %), next-30 only. Not done yet: Orange4Home (email-only dataset), the clustering task, the paper's other baseline models.

Paper: Fiori et al., arXiv 2602.01910. Full list with paper sections: docs/DOMUSFM_REPRODUCTION.md ('What follows the paper', 'Assumptions', run 3). Why a later-time test is stricter: consecutive windows share 29 of 30 events, so a random split puts near-copies of each test window in training.
